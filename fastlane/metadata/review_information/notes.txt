Day is a free, read-only client for Google Calendar. It has no account of its own: signing in is Google's own sign-in, through a system web authentication session.

How to test:

1. Open Day. The first screen has one button, Add Google Account.
2. Sign in with the demo account given in the review information fields. It is a throwaway Google account that holds only invented events.
3. Day opens on today. Swipe sideways for other days, or go back to the month.
4. Press and hold the Lock Screen, choose Customize, and add the Next widget above or below the clock.

Day asks Google for read-only access to calendars (the calendar.readonly scope) and for the account's email address. It cannot create, change or delete events. Open in Google Calendar hands the event's link to the Google Calendar app or to Safari.

Privacy: Day reads your calendars from Google and keeps them on your devices. Your sign-in moves between them in iCloud Keychain, which is end-to-end encrypted. To show changes as they happen, Google tells Day's server at apps.idea2prod.in that a calendar changed, never what changed, and the server keeps only random IDs, push addresses and the device type. App Store privacy label: Data Not Linked to You (a device identifier, used to deliver updates to your calendars). app/PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy in the open source repository (https://github.com/spashii/day) says the same.

No purchase is needed for any feature. Day has no ads, no analytics and no tracking.
